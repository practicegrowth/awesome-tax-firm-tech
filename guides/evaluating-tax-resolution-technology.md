# Evaluating tax-resolution technology

This guide is a vendor-neutral worksheet for tax-resolution, CPA, tax, and accounting firms evaluating case-management, transcript, client-communication, or workflow technology. It does not determine eligibility to practice, authorization status, tax outcomes, security compliance, or the suitability of a product for a particular client matter.

Use current IRS materials, vendor documentation, the firm's own policies, and appropriate professional advice for the actual engagement.

## 1. Separate IRS access from vendor workflow claims

Document what the product does and what still requires an eligible, authorized person using IRS systems. Do not assume a product provides IRS access, authorization, legal representation, or a particular result because it can store documents or analyze data.

| Review point | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- |
| Intended workflow | Intake, authorization, transcript review, notice tracking, case planning, client communication, billing, or closure. |
| IRS-facing step | The applicable IRS service, required authorization, credential, and accountable practitioner or staff role. |
| Vendor workflow step | The exact vendor feature, product tier, data source, and limitations shown in current documentation. |
| Human decision point | The person who reviews information, determines next steps, and approves any client or IRS communication. |
| Record and exit plan | How case records, documents, notes, and audit evidence are preserved or exported if the tool changes. |

The IRS states that use of the Transcript Delivery System requires an appropriate Form 2848 or Form 8821 on file, and that eligible users access it with IRS credentials. Verify the current requirements directly with the IRS for the relevant matter. [IRS TDS](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/transcript-delivery-system-tds)

## 2. Test the actual case handoffs

Choose representative, firm-approved scenarios and test the complete handoff rather than only a product demonstration.

| Scenario | Questions to test |
| --- | --- |
| New inquiry | Is the scope communicated clearly, and does the workflow avoid promising an outcome before review? |
| Authorization collection | Who verifies form completeness, taxpayer identity, signatures, and status before any IRS-facing step? |
| Transcript or notice review | Can the reviewer identify the source, date, taxpayer, tax period, and unresolved questions? |
| Client request | Does the workflow preserve a clear record, route sensitive questions to the right person, and avoid unreviewed conclusions? |
| Escalation or exception | What happens when authorization is absent, data is incomplete, a deadline is uncertain, or the system is unavailable? |
| Closure or transition | Who confirms final client communication, retention obligations, billing status, and access removal? |

## 3. Ask vendors for specific, current evidence

For each shortlisted product, retain the date and URL for the exact plan or feature under consideration.

- What data is entered, imported, generated, retained, and exported—and under whose permissions?
- Which features rely on firm-entered information, IRS access, third-party integrations, or vendor-generated analysis?
- How are access roles, activity history, document sharing, and offboarding documented?
- What are the current support, implementation, incident, data-export, and contract terms?
- What remains the firm's responsibility for authorization, practitioner judgment, communication, security review, and recordkeeping?

Do not infer integrations, security controls, compliance posture, case outcomes, or time savings from a marketing page or demonstration.

## 4. Record a bounded pilot decision

Set a named owner, a limited workflow, pilot dates, review criteria, and a rollback plan. At the end, record the evidence reviewed, observed failures, unresolved risks, commercial assumptions, and one decision: **adopt for the defined workflow**, **extend with specific controls**, or **stop**.

## Primary-source references

- The IRS explains [Form 2848](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-2848) as an authorization for an eligible individual to represent a taxpayer before the IRS.
- The IRS provides current [Form 2848 and Form 8821 submission options](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/submit-forms-2848-and-8821-online), including Tax Pro Account and online form submission.
- The IRS Office of Professional Responsibility describes [Circular 230](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/office-of-professional-responsibility-and-circular-230) as governing practice before the IRS for covered tax professionals. These resources do not replace the current rules, instructions, or professional advice applicable to a specific matter.
