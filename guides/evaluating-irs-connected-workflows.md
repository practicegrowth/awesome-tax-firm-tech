# Evaluating IRS-connected workflows

This vendor-neutral worksheet helps tax, CPA, accounting, bookkeeping, and tax-resolution firms evaluate technology that stores, routes, or supports work involving IRS authorizations, transcripts, notices, payments, or tax-account information. It does not determine whether a person is authorized to act, whether an authorization is valid, how a matter should be handled, or whether a product meets a firm's legal, tax, privacy, security, or compliance obligations.

Use current IRS materials, the firm's own policies, vendor documentation, and appropriate professional advice for the actual client matter.

## 1. Map the official system and the supporting workflow separately

Start with the IRS service that performs the official action. Then identify the firm and vendor steps that prepare, document, or follow up on that action. A workflow platform is not a substitute for IRS credentials, authorization, or practitioner judgment.

| Review point | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- |
| Client purpose | The specific firm-approved purpose, taxpayer or entity type, period, and staff owner. |
| Official IRS step | The applicable IRS service, its current eligibility requirements, and the responsible individual who performs the step. |
| Authorization status | The applicable form or authorization path, its status, source, date, and reviewer. |
| Supporting technology | The exact vendor feature, product tier, data inputs, permissions, and limitations shown in current documentation. |
| Review and record | Who verifies the output, where it is retained, how corrections are handled, and how access is removed at closure. |

For example, the IRS says eligible EROs and Circular 230 practitioners can use the Transcript Delivery System (TDS) for specified client information when a properly executed Form 2848 or Form 8821 is on file. Confirm the current IRS requirements for the actual service and matter rather than inferring them from a vendor workflow. [IRS TDS](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/transcript-delivery-system-tds)

## 2. Test authorization-to-record handoffs

Run a small, firm-approved test using representative but appropriately protected records. Test the whole handoff, not only a software demonstration.

| Scenario | Questions to test |
| --- | --- |
| Authorization intake | Who checks the source, completeness, taxpayer details, signatures, scope, and current status before an IRS-facing request? |
| IRS request | Does the process identify the account used, the person performing the action, the date and time, and the resulting record? |
| Document or data receipt | Can staff distinguish an IRS-produced record from a vendor-generated summary, preserve the source and period, and route it to the right reviewer? |
| Client communication | Who approves the message, what source supports it, and how are unanswered questions or time-sensitive items escalated? |
| Correction, exception, or closure | What happens when an authorization is absent, a record conflicts with firm data, access fails, or the engagement ends? |

The IRS lists Tax Pro Account, online submission, and mail or fax as options for submitting Forms 2848 and 8821; the appropriate path depends on the current IRS rules and the matter. [IRS submission options](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/submit-forms-2848-and-8821-online)

## 3. Require provider evidence without treating it as an IRS claim

For each product considered, record the date and URL for the exact plan or feature. Ask the provider to distinguish its workflow capabilities from any official IRS system or firm responsibility.

- What information is entered, imported, generated, retained, exported, or shared, and under whose permissions?
- Does the feature rely on staff-entered data, a firm-held authorization, an IRS service, a third-party connection, or vendor-generated analysis?
- How are user roles, activity history, document access, exports, and offboarding documented?
- What are the current implementation, support, incident, data-export, and contract terms?
- What remains the firm's responsibility for authorization, access control, review, client communication, retention, and escalation?

Do not infer that a provider can request records from the IRS, validate authorization, provide representation, or deliver a particular client outcome unless the applicable official and provider documentation clearly establish the narrow claim.

## 4. Apply taxpayer-data controls to the workflow

Treat IRS-connected work as a taxpayer-data workflow even when a vendor handles only intake, tasks, or document storage. Identify the minimum roles that need access; use individual accounts; retain a clear activity record; and test the process for removing access when work or employment changes. The IRS recommends unique credentials and multi-factor authentication for accounts and systems used by tax professionals, among other safeguards. [IRS Publication 4557](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf)

Document any unresolved question about access, storage, exports, incident response, or retention for the firm to review under its own policies and applicable requirements.

## 5. Record a bounded adoption decision

Give the pilot a named owner, defined IRS-connected workflow, limited records, dates, success criteria, and a rollback plan. At the end, record the official sources and provider evidence reviewed, observed failures, unresolved risks, commercial assumptions, and one decision: **adopt for the defined workflow**, **extend with specific controls**, or **stop**.

## Primary-source references

- The IRS describes [e-Services](https://www.irs.gov/e-services) as a suite of web-based tools for tax professionals and identifies TDS, TIN matching, e-file services, and other services separately.
- The IRS explains [TDS](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/transcript-delivery-system-tds), its eligible users, available records, and authorization prerequisites.
- The IRS provides [Tax Pro Account and authorization submission options](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/serve-your-clients) for tax professionals. Verify current availability and eligibility directly with the IRS.
- The IRS's [Publication 4557](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf) provides taxpayer-data safeguard guidance for tax professionals. These references do not replace current IRS instructions, firm policies, or professional advice for a specific engagement.
