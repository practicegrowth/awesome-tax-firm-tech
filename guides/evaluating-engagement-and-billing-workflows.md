# Evaluating engagement and billing workflows

This vendor-neutral worksheet helps tax, CPA, accounting, and bookkeeping firms evaluate proposal, engagement-letter, billing, payment-request, and client-approval workflows. It focuses on the operational record each workflow creates, the people accountable for each handoff, and the evidence needed before broad adoption.

It does not determine engagement scope, fee arrangements, payment obligations, e-signature enforceability, tax treatment, professional standards, or legal compliance. Use the firm's approved engagement materials, current vendor documentation, and appropriate professional advice for the engagement and jurisdiction.

## 1. Start with the firm's actual engagement path

Document a representative service before comparing products. Include the people, data, approval points, and records that exist before and after the client sees a proposal or payment request.

| Stage | Evidence to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Scope and proposal | Who approves the described service, fee, assumptions, exclusions, and client-facing version before sending? |
| Client review | What the client receives, how questions and requested changes are recorded, and who owns follow-up? |
| Approval or signature | Which event marks approval, what record is retained, and what happens if the document changes afterward? |
| Billing and payment request | When the request is issued, what amount and service period it represents, and who can change, void, or resend it? |
| Reconciliation and exceptions | How the firm identifies payment status, failed payments, disputes, credits, refunds, and items requiring human review. |
| Retention and exit | Where the agreement, communications, billing history, and supporting documents are retained and how the firm retrieves them. |

## 2. Separate firm decisions from product behavior

For each proposed workflow, identify the decisions the firm makes and the actions the product records or automates. Do not assume a tool defines the firm's scope, authorizes a charge, resolves a dispute, or supplies a complete business record merely because it can send a proposal, collect a signature, or request payment.

Ask:

- Which roles can draft, approve, send, edit, cancel, refund, or export an engagement or payment record?
- What happens to an in-flight proposal when pricing, scope, a contact, or staff ownership changes?
- Which workflow steps are automated, and what review or escalation occurs before external communication or a financial action?
- What data is passed to accounting, practice-management, payment, or client-portal systems, and who verifies exceptions?
- Can the firm reconstruct the timeline of a client request, approval, message, invoice, payment status, and subsequent change?

## 3. Request plan-specific provider evidence

For each finalist, retain the date, URL, plan name, agreement, and implementation notes for the specific features under consideration.

| Topic | Questions to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Workflow ownership | What tasks, notifications, approvals, and exception handling remain the firm's responsibility? |
| Roles and history | What roles can act on records, what activity history is available, and can the firm export it? |
| Data flow | What information is stored, transmitted, integrated, retained, or deleted, and under which account or permissions? |
| Support and incidents | How are suspected misdelivery, duplicate requests, payment disputes, loss of access, or service outages reported and handled? |
| Transition | What current options exist for exporting records, preserving client communications, changing providers, and removing access? |

Do not infer integrations, payment-processing terms, security controls, retention periods, audit history, implementation scope, acceptance rates, time savings, or commercial outcomes from a product tour or marketing page.

## 4. Run a bounded pilot

Select one firm-approved use case—for example, a new tax engagement, recurring bookkeeping billing cycle, or advisory proposal. Use a limited participant group, named owner, pilot dates, and clear review criteria.

Test at least one normal path and one exception: a scope change, a client question, a failed payment, a revised proposal, or a staff reassignment. At the end, record the evidence reviewed, observed gaps, unresolved assumptions, required firm controls, and one decision: **adopt for the defined workflow**, **extend with named controls**, or **stop**.

## 5. Protect taxpayer and client information throughout the workflow

Engagement and billing workflows may handle taxpayer or other sensitive client information. The IRS recommends limiting access to people with a business need, using multifactor authentication for people accessing customer information, and maintaining activity records. Apply those considerations to the whole handoff, including the firm's staff practices and connected systems—not as an unsupported claim about a provider. [IRS Publication 4557](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf)

## Primary-source reference

- [IRS Publication 4557, Safeguarding Taxpayer Data](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf) discusses access limits, multifactor authentication, activity records, phishing, and data-loss response for tax professionals. It is a starting point for a firm-specific security program, not a product certification or a substitute for professional advice.
