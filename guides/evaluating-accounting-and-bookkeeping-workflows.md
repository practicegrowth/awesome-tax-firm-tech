# Evaluating accounting, bookkeeping, and advisory workflows

This worksheet helps a CPA, accounting, bookkeeping, or tax firm evaluate a defined workflow for source-document intake, transaction processing, close, reporting, or advisory handoff. It is not accounting, tax, legal, compliance, security, or assurance advice. It does not establish a client-service standard, a reporting conclusion, or a product capability.

## 1. Define the workflow and service boundary

Write down the exact work under review: for example, receipt collection through categorization, month-end close through client review, or data preparation through an advisory meeting. Identify the accountable owner, client responsibilities, source systems, approval points, outputs, exceptions, and the end-of-service handoff.

| Question | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- |
| What enters the workflow? | Source-document types, import sources, client responsibilities, and timing assumptions. |
| What is the service boundary? | Firm-approved scope, exclusions, escalation rule, and client-facing description. |
| Who performs and reviews each stage? | Role matrix, access record, review evidence, and coverage plan. |
| What leaves the workflow? | Defined deliverable, delivery path, period, version, and client confirmation or next action. |
| How are exceptions handled? | Missing-information, duplicate, correction, approval, and deadline handoffs. |

## 2. Test the end-to-end handoff

Use approved representative scenarios rather than relying only on a product demonstration. Include a complete source-document set, a late or missing document, a duplicate or mismatched item, a correction after review, an access change, and a client question that falls outside the defined scope.

| Test area | Questions to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Source capture | Can the firm distinguish the source, period, client, and status of each item without creating duplicate work? |
| Processing and review | Are assumptions, changes, unresolved items, and reviewer decisions visible to the right people? |
| Close and reporting | Can the firm identify which period, data state, and review point produced the deliverable? |
| Client handoff | Does the client receive the intended output, context, and next action through the approved channel? |
| Change control | Can the firm trace a correction, owner, reason, and resulting client communication? |
| Offboarding | Can access and data handoffs be completed according to the firm’s policy and its provider documentation? |

## 3. Ask vendors for exact evidence

For each product or integration, retain the date and URL for the exact product tier and workflow under review. Ask for current documentation covering source capture, imports and exports, roles and permissions, review or approval controls, audit or activity records where applicable, client access, error handling, support escalation, retention or deletion, and contract terms.

Do not infer that a platform reconciles a particular account, supports a particular accounting treatment, provides an assurance control, meets a security requirement, or integrates with another product based on a demonstration or a marketing page.

## 4. Treat client and taxpayer data as a workflow requirement

Map what information is collected, viewed, changed, exported, or shared at each step. Define the minimum roles that need access, the approved channels for client exchange, and what triggers a security or privacy escalation. Keep the firm’s service decisions separate from the provider’s documented capabilities.

The IRS publishes resources for tax professionals on safeguarding taxpayer data, and the FTC provides Safeguards Rule guidance for covered financial institutions. Use the current primary sources and appropriate professional advice to determine obligations that apply to the firm; this worksheet does not determine coverage or compliance. [IRS Publication 4557](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf) · [FTC Safeguards Rule guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know)

## 5. Make a bounded decision

Record the workflow scope, owner, products reviewed, source evidence, representative tests, unresolved risks, commercial assumptions, and one explicit outcome:

- **Adopt** for the defined workflow with named controls and a review date.
- **Extend** testing after specified evidence or process changes are completed.
- **Stop** when the workflow cannot be validated or supported within the firm’s service boundary.

Revisit the decision when the firm changes scope, clients, source systems, access roles, providers, or close procedures, and before the next high-volume period.

## 6. Escalate instead of guessing

Obtain appropriate review when a change affects client data, confidential taxpayer information, service-scope representations, professional standards, a disputed financial treatment, regulated advertising, a security incident, or an access concern. Do not use this worksheet to decide a client accounting or tax position.
