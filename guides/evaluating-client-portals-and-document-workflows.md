# Evaluating client portals and document workflows

This vendor-neutral worksheet helps tax, CPA, accounting, and bookkeeping firms evaluate client portals, document-request systems, e-signature workflows, and related practice-management features. It is designed to test the client journey and the firm's operating controls together—not to select a vendor based on a feature checklist alone.

It does not determine whether a product or workflow meets a legal, regulatory, contractual, privacy, or security obligation. Use current official guidance, firm policies, vendor documentation, and appropriate professional advice for the firm's circumstances.

## 1. Map the complete client handoff

Start with one representative engagement and document the information flow before comparing products.

| Stage | Evidence to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Invitation | What the client receives, who sends it, how the intended recipient is confirmed, and what support path is provided. |
| Intake and requests | The documents, questions, deadlines, reminders, owners, and escalation path for incomplete or unclear submissions. |
| Review and follow-up | Who can view submissions, what context accompanies a file, and how open questions are recorded and routed. |
| Signature or approval | The specific firm approval step, evidence retained, and what happens if the signer or document changes. |
| Delivery and closure | What the client can access, the retention and access-removal steps, and how a former client requests records. |

The IRS recommends limiting access to taxpayer data to people with a business need, using multifactor authentication for people accessing customer information, and maintaining activity records. Treat those as workflow questions for the firm and provider, not claims about any product. [IRS Publication 4557](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf)

## 2. Test the client experience and the firm controls together

Use a firm-approved, nonproduction scenario if possible. Test the actions a client and staff member actually take rather than relying only on a demonstration.

- Can a client understand the request, submit the right item, and ask for help without exposing information through an unapproved channel?
- Can the firm identify who sent, viewed, changed, downloaded, or approved an item when that record matters?
- Can a staff member correct a request, change a due date, or reassign an item without losing context or ownership?
- What access remains when a client, team member, contractor, or partner changes roles or leaves?
- Can the firm recover a document, export the relevant records, or continue essential communication if the service is unavailable?

Do not use production taxpayer information in a pilot unless the firm has approved the workflow and safeguards for that purpose.

## 3. Ask for plan-specific provider evidence

For each finalist, retain the date, URL, plan name, and contract materials for the features actually under consideration.

| Topic | Questions to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Access and permissions | What roles exist, how are they administered, and which actions can each role take? |
| Data flow | Where is information entered, stored, shared, exported, and deleted? Which actions require another product or service? |
| Records and activity | What event history is available, who can see it, how long is it retained, and can the firm export it? |
| Support and exceptions | How are suspected misdelivery, lost access, outages, or disputed signatures handled and documented? |
| Transition | What current options exist for data export, client communication, access removal, and contract exit? |

Do not infer encryption, storage location, integrations, audit history, retention, security certifications, e-signature validity, implementation scope, or client-adoption outcomes from a sales page or product tour.

## 4. Define a bounded pilot and its exit decision

Choose one workflow—such as organizer collection, new-client onboarding, engagement approval, or return delivery. Name an owner, a limited participant group, pilot dates, success evidence, known risks, and a rollback path.

At the end, record one decision: **adopt for the defined workflow**, **extend with named controls**, or **stop**. Include unresolved questions, staff training needs, client-support requirements, provider terms to confirm, and a review date.

## Primary-source reference

- [IRS Publication 4557, Safeguarding Taxpayer Data](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf) discusses access limits, multifactor authentication, encryption, audit trails, phishing, and data-loss response for tax professionals. It is a starting point for a firm-specific security program, not a product certification or a substitute for professional advice.
