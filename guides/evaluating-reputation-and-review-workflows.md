# Evaluating reputation and review workflows for tax and accounting firms

This worksheet helps a tax, CPA, accounting, bookkeeping, or tax-resolution firm evaluate a defined review-request, review-monitoring, or response workflow. It is not a promise of reviews, rankings, leads, or revenue. It does not replace the firm’s privacy, advertising, ethics, client-communication, or platform-policy review.

## 1. Define the workflow before choosing a tool

Describe the client moment that may trigger an invitation, the eligible service, the channel, the accountable staff member, the platform, and the stop or escalation condition. Keep voluntary feedback requests distinct from engagement, tax, or representation work.

| Question | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- |
| What client interaction starts the workflow? | Firm-approved trigger, timing, and exclusions. |
| Who is eligible to receive an invitation? | Documented rule that does not screen for expected sentiment. |
| What is the exact request and destination? | Approved template, platform link or QR code, and date reviewed. |
| Who can monitor and respond? | Named roles, access record, response approvals, and escalation route. |
| What information must never be disclosed? | Firm privacy rule and response redaction/escalation procedure. |

Google’s current guidance says reviews should reflect a genuine experience and prohibits offering incentives in exchange for reviews, changed reviews, or removal of negative reviews. Confirm the current rules of each platform used before launching or changing a workflow. [Google Business Profile: tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)

## 2. Test the client journey and response controls

Run a small, approved test using representative, non-client scenarios where possible. Check that the request is accurate, voluntary, and accessible; that the destination works; and that staff can recognize matters that need escalation rather than a public reply.

| Test area | Questions to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Eligibility | Does the workflow apply the documented rule consistently, without filtering for favorable feedback? |
| Request | Does the message avoid incentives, misleading language, and unnecessary client or matter details? |
| Public profile | Is the firm’s business name, service area or address, category, contact information, and hours accurate? |
| Monitoring | Can the owner identify new feedback, platform notices, or access changes without sharing credentials? |
| Response | Does the draft acknowledge feedback without confirming a client relationship, matter, financial information, or other confidential detail? |
| Escalation | Is there a clear path for complaints, suspected policy violations, legal concerns, and privacy-sensitive feedback? |

Google requires Business Profile information to represent the real-world business accurately and says one profile per business is generally appropriate. Its guidance also states that a business must be verified before it can reply to reviews. [Google Business Profile representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) · [Manage customer reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050)

## 3. Measure operations, not a promised outcome

Use a stable review period and record operational evidence separately from business results. Examples of useful operational measures include invitations sent under the approved rule, delivery failures, reviews requiring escalation, response time against the firm’s own target, unresolved access issues, and corrections to inaccurate public information.

Do not treat review count, rating, profile views, calls, rankings, or revenue as a guaranteed consequence of a workflow change. Keep platform data, website analytics, and intake records separate unless the firm has defined their collection, consent, and attribution limits.

## 4. Record an adoption decision

For a new tool or changed process, preserve the workflow scope, vendor or platform evidence, configuration owner, test results, open risks, commercial assumptions, and one explicit decision:

- **Adopt** for the defined workflow and review date.
- **Extend** only after named controls or evidence are completed.
- **Stop** when the workflow cannot be operated consistently, safely, or within applicable policy.

Re-check the workflow after platform-policy changes, significant complaints, changes in firm services or locations, an access incident, or before the next busy season.

## 5. Escalate instead of guessing

Obtain appropriate review before responding publicly to feedback involving a client matter, taxpayer information, a disputed professional-service claim, an incentive, a threat, a legal demand, an alleged policy violation, or a regulated-advertising or privacy question. Do not use a public response to confirm that someone is or was a client.
