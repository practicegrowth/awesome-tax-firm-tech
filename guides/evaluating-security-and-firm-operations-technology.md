# Evaluating security and firm-operations technology

This vendor-neutral worksheet helps tax, CPA, accounting, and bookkeeping firms evaluate tools or providers that affect access to taxpayer information: for example, password management, identity and access management, hosted desktops, managed IT, backup, document platforms, and security monitoring.

It does not determine whether a product, provider, or firm satisfies a legal, regulatory, insurance, contractual, or security requirement. Use current official guidance, the firm's written information-security program, vendor documentation, and appropriate professional advice for the firm's circumstances.

## 1. Start with the protected workflow, not a product feature list

For each proposed tool or provider, record the exact workflow before comparing features.

| Review point | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- |
| Information involved | The types of taxpayer or firm information, where it originates, and whether it is stored, viewed, transmitted, or exported. |
| People and access | Named roles that administer, use, support, or can recover access to the system. |
| Systems and handoffs | Devices, accounts, integrations, file transfers, email, portals, and service providers involved. |
| Firm control | The settings, approvals, training, monitoring, and offboarding steps the firm—not the vendor—must perform. |
| Failure scenario | Lost device, compromised account, unavailable service, misdirected file, staff departure, or suspected security event. |

The IRS recommends inventorying the devices and software that store, process, or transmit taxpayer data, limiting access to those with a business need, and using activity records. [IRS Publication 4557](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf)

## 2. Evaluate identity, access, and recovery in the real workflow

Ask each candidate provider for current, plan-specific documentation and test a representative firm-approved scenario.

- How are individual accounts created, changed, reviewed, and removed when a worker changes roles or leaves?
- Which access roles can view, download, share, export, delete, or administer taxpayer information?
- Is multifactor authentication available for the workflow under consideration, and who configures and monitors it?
- What activity history is available to the firm, how long is it retained, and how can it be exported?
- Who can reset an administrator account or bypass normal access, and what evidence is produced when that happens?
- What happens to firm data and access during an outage, a disputed account, or a provider transition?

Do not infer access controls, encryption, monitoring, retention, backup, incident support, or compliance status from a sales page, badge, questionnaire, or product demonstration. Retain the relevant documentation, effective date, contract terms, and test result for the plan actually being considered.

## 3. Evaluate the provider relationship separately from the product

Technology may support a firm's safeguards, but it does not transfer the firm's accountability for its own program. For each provider, capture:

| Topic | Questions to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | What information and systems does the provider handle, and what is explicitly out of scope? |
| Responsibilities | Which controls are configured and operated by the provider, and which remain with the firm? |
| Support and incidents | How does the firm report a suspected event, who is notified, what evidence is provided, and what response commitments apply? |
| Data lifecycle | What are the current export, return, deletion, retention, and transition options? |
| Oversight | What current evidence can the firm review, and how will it periodically reassess the relationship? |

The FTC's Safeguards Rule guidance describes a written information-security program that is appropriate to the business and requires covered businesses to oversee service providers. The IRS's tax-practice WISP template is a practical starting point for smaller practices; neither source substitutes for a firm-specific assessment. [FTC Safeguards Rule overview](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know) · [IRS Publication 5708](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5708.pdf)

## 4. Test one bounded scenario before broad rollout

Use a limited, firm-approved pilot that includes normal use and a failure or offboarding step. Record the date, participants, system configuration, evidence reviewed, observed gaps, and the decision.

| Scenario | What to test |
| --- | --- |
| New staff member | Provision only the necessary access; confirm the access record and training responsibility. |
| Sensitive client file | Follow the intended storage, sharing, and review workflow without using production client data unless the firm has approved it. |
| Access removal | Remove a departing or reassigned user's access and verify the remaining ownership, audit record, and recovery path. |
| Suspected incident | Confirm the firm's escalation contact, provider contact path, preserved evidence, and next decision maker. |
| Provider transition | Test whether the firm can identify and retrieve the records and settings needed for an orderly exit. |

## 5. Make a documented decision

At the end of the review, record one outcome: **adopt for the defined workflow**, **extend the pilot with named controls**, or **stop**. Include unresolved assumptions, commercial terms to confirm, required firm actions, and the next review date. Revisit the decision when the workflow, provider terms, firm systems, or relevant threats change.

## Primary-source references

- [IRS Publication 4557, Safeguarding Taxpayer Data](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf) discusses safeguards for tax professionals, including multifactor authentication, access limits, encryption, activity records, and incident response resources.
- [IRS Publication 5708, Creating a Written Information Security Plan for Your Tax & Accounting Practice](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5708.pdf) provides a sample WISP for tax and accounting practices and states that it is not exhaustive or a substitute for a firm-specific plan.
- [FTC Safeguards Rule: What Your Business Needs to Know](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know) explains the FTC's information-security-program guidance for covered financial institutions, including tax preparation firms.
