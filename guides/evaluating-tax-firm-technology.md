# Evaluating tax-firm technology

This guide is a practical, vendor-neutral starting point for tax, accounting, bookkeeping, CPA, and tax-resolution firms evaluating a product or workflow change. It is not a ranking, implementation prescription, tax, legal, accounting, compliance, or security opinion.

Use the vendor's current documentation, contract, and support materials to confirm claims that matter to the firm. A listing in this repository is not evidence that a product meets any particular requirement.

## 1. Define the decision before comparing products

Write a one-sentence decision statement before booking demonstrations. For example: *Reduce return-status follow-up without weakening client communication or review controls.*

Then record:

- **Workflow and owner:** the specific process, its accountable owner, and the affected roles.
- **Baseline:** current volume, cycle time, rework, client follow-up, or other measure that explains the problem.
- **Non-negotiables:** firm-approved requirements such as required tax forms, client experience, data handling, access controls, integrations, or budget boundaries.
- **Exit condition:** how the firm will export data, stop the pilot, or change tools if the product is not a fit.

## 2. Gather evidence, not just feature claims

For each shortlisted product, retain the date and URL of the source used. Ask the vendor to point to current documentation for the exact product tier and use case under consideration.

| Evidence to capture | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Official product and integration documentation | Clarifies what is documented rather than assumed from a demo. |
| Current pricing, order form, and renewal terms | Separates published pricing from the proposed commercial commitment. |
| Data export, retention, and deletion documentation | Helps the firm plan continuity and a possible exit. |
| Identity, permissions, and security documentation | Supports the firm's own security review; it is not a substitute for one. |
| Implementation, support, and service-level materials | Makes ownership, timing, and escalation expectations explicit. |

Do not infer an integration, certification, security control, tax outcome, or compliance posture from a marketing page. Confirm material items directly with the provider and the firm's appropriate advisers.

## 3. Use a consistent decision scorecard

Score each criterion on a simple 0–2 scale: **0** not demonstrated or unsuitable, **1** partially demonstrated or requires mitigation, **2** demonstrated and suitable for the intended use. Add a short source note beside every score.

| Criterion | Questions to test |
| --- | --- |
| Workflow fit | Does the product support the actual handoffs, review points, exceptions, and seasonal volume? |
| Client experience | Can clients complete the required action without unnecessary accounts, friction, or ambiguity? |
| Adoption and administration | Who configures it, trains the team, manages access, and keeps it current? |
| Data and integrations | What data moves, in which direction, under whose permissions, and how is it exported? |
| Controls and risk | What controls are documented, what remains the firm's responsibility, and what must be independently validated? |
| Commercial fit | What are the total expected costs, contract commitments, usage limits, and likely implementation effort? |

Use the scorecard to expose tradeoffs, not to manufacture a universal winner. A firm serving high-volume individual returns may reasonably choose differently from one handling complex entity or controversy work.

## 4. Category-specific review prompts

### Tax preparation and workflow

- Confirm the return types, forms, jurisdictions, e-file workflow, diagnostics, review controls, and data-import paths the firm actually needs.
- Test a representative complex return and an exception case, not only a clean demonstration file.
- Establish who owns return status, review queues, and filing confirmations.

### Practice management, portals, and document collection

- Map the client request, reminder, document receipt, review, and closure flow end to end.
- Test external-user access, role permissions, notifications, document retrieval, and offboarding.
- Verify how data is exported if the firm changes systems.

### Payments, billing, and engagement

- Confirm the payment flow, reconciliation responsibility, engagement workflow, and client communication before launch.
- Review the provider's current terms and the firm's professional, regulatory, and adviser requirements for the intended workflow.

### AI and automation

- Define a bounded use case, its human owner, acceptable inputs, and required human review before enabling it.
- Test outputs against representative firm work and document failure modes, escalation paths, and prohibited uses.
- Treat AI-generated output as draft material unless a qualified person has reviewed it for the intended purpose.

NIST's voluntary [AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) can be a useful reference for structuring an organization’s own AI-risk work; it is not a tax-firm-specific certification or checklist.

### Security and firm operations

- Use the firm's security review process to evaluate access, identity, device, logging, backup, incident-response, and vendor-management needs.
- Obtain current documentation from the provider and involve the appropriate technical, legal, compliance, or insurance advisers where needed.

## 5. Run a bounded pilot and record the decision

Define a pilot that has a named owner, limited participants, a defined workflow, a start and end date, and a rollback plan. At the end, record what worked, what failed, measured impact against the baseline, unresolved risks, commercial assumptions, and the decision: adopt, extend the pilot, or stop.

Keeping this decision record makes future renewal, migration, and process-improvement work substantially easier.
